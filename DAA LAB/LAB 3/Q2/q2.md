# Question 2: Search the Defective Coin

## Problem Statement
As a quality-control engineer, the task is to identify a single potentially defective (lighter) coin out of $n$ identically appearing coins, or verify if all coins are perfectly weighted. 
We are provided with a balance weighing scale. The critical constraint is to design a **Divide and Conquer** algorithm that finds the defective coin (if it exists) in strictly **$O(\log_2 n + c)$** time, where $c$ is a positive constant.

---

## Algorithmic Approach & Theoretical Justification

### The "Catch" in the Problem Statement
Traditional coin-weighing puzzles aim for the absolute minimum number of weighings by dividing the pile into 3 equal parts, yielding a time complexity of $O(\log_3 n)$. However, the problem statement strictly demands an **$O(\log_2 n + c)$** time complexity constraint. 

To satisfy a base-2 logarithmic bound, our Divide and Conquer algorithm must split the search space (the pile of coins) strictly into **two equal halves** at each recursive step, similar to Binary Search.

### The 2-Way Divide and Conquer Logic
1. **Base Case:** If only 1 coin remains, it is the target search space.
2. **Even Number of Coins ($n$ is even):**
   * Divide the coins into exactly two equal halves.
   * Weigh the two halves against each other.
   * If they balance out, there is **no defective coin** in the batch.
   * If one side is lighter, the defective coin is in that half. Recurse on the lighter half.
3. **Odd Number of Coins ($n$ is odd):**
   * Keep one coin aside.
   * Divide the remaining even number of coins into two equal halves and weigh them.
   * If the scale balances, the **coin kept aside** is the defective one.
   * If one side is lighter, the defective coin is in that half. Recurse on the lighter half.

### Complexity Analysis
In every step, we perform exactly one weighing operation $O(1)$ and reduce our search space strictly by a factor of 2 ($N/2$). 
The recurrence relation is:
$$T(N) = T(N/2) + O(1)$$
According to the Master Theorem, this results in a time complexity of exactly **$O(\log_2 n)$**, strictly satisfying the problem's mathematical constraint.
