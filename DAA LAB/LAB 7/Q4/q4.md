# Question 4: Security Switches

## Problem Statement
A row of $n$ security switches protects a military installation entrance. Switches can be toggled subject to the following rules:
1. The rightmost switch may be turned on or off at will.
2. Any other switch may be turned on or off only if the switch to its immediate right is on and all the other switches to its right, if any, are off.
3. Only one switch may be toggled at a time.

Devise an algorithm to turn off all switches, which are initially all on, in the minimum number of moves. Write a C program to validate the procedure and analyze the complexity.

---

## Algorithm & Mathematical Formulation

### 1. Problem Mapping
This problem is mathematically isomorphic to the **Baguenaudier puzzle** (also known as Chinese Rings) and binary reflected Gray codes.

Let:
* $F(n)$ denote the number of operations required to turn **OFF** $n$ switches given that they are all initially ON.
* $S(n)$ denote the number of operations required to turn **ON** $n$ switches given that they are all initially OFF.

### 2. Recursive Rules
To turn off switch $n$, switch $n-1$ must be ON and switches $1, \dots, n-2$ must be OFF. Starting with all $n$ switches ON:
1. Turn off switches $1, \dots, n-2 \implies F(n-2)$ moves.
2. Toggle switch $n$ from ON to OFF $\implies 1$ move.
3. Turn on switches $1, \dots, n-2 \implies S(n-2)$ moves (to allow shutting down switch $n-1$).
4. Turn off switches $1, \dots, n-1 \implies F(n-1)$ moves.

Since turning off and turning on are symmetric operations ($F(k) = S(k)$):
$$F(n) = F(n-1) + 2F(n-2) + 1$$

### 3. Closed-Form Minimum Moves
The sequence of moves $M(n)$ required to turn off $n$ all-ON switches evaluates to:
$$M(n) = \begin{cases} 
\frac{2^{n+1} - 2}{3}, & \text{if } n \text{ is even} \\[8pt]
\frac{2^{n+1} - 1}{3}, & \text{if } n \text{ is odd}
\end{cases}$$

For example:
* $n = 1 \implies M(1) = 1$ move.
* $n = 2 \implies M(2) = 2$ moves.
* $n = 3 \implies M(3) = 5$ moves.
* $n = 4 \implies M(4) = 10$ moves.

---

## Complexity Analysis
* **Time Complexity:** $\Theta(2^n)$. The recurrence relation $F(n) = F(n-1) + 2F(n-2) + 1$ yields an exact exponential solution proportional to $\frac{2^{n+1}}{3}$.
* **Space Complexity:** $O(n)$ recursion stack frames corresponding to the maximum depth of the call chain.
