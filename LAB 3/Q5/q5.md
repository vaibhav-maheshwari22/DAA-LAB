# Question 5: Multiply Special-Pattern Matrices ($O(n^2)$ Approach)

## Problem Statement
Two $n \times n$ matrices are provided where $n = 2^k$. Each matrix has a recursive structure where, when divided into four equal-sized blocks, two diagonal blocks are identical, and two off-diagonal blocks are identical. 
Give a divide-and-conquer approach-based $O(n^2)$ algorithm for multiplying two such matrices and validate the complexity.

---

## Theoretical Justification & Algorithm

Let the input matrices $A$ and $B$ have the special recursive structure:
$$A = \begin{bmatrix} M_1 & M_2 \\ M_2 & M_1 \end{bmatrix}$$
$$B = \begin{bmatrix} N_1 & N_2 \\ N_2 & N_1 \end{bmatrix}$$

Multiplying these will inherently result in a matrix $C$ of the exact same symmetric structure:
$$C = \begin{bmatrix} C_1 & C_2 \\ C_2 & C_1 \end{bmatrix}$$

Because of this symmetry, we do not need to calculate all 4 quadrants independently (which would normally take 8 recursive multiplications). Instead, we can calculate $C_1$ and $C_2$ using just **2 recursive multiplications**:

1. Let $U = (M_1 + M_2) \times (N_1 + N_2)$
2. Let $V = (M_1 - M_2) \times (N_1 - N_2)$

Then, the final values of the quadrants are:
* $C_1 = (U + V) / 2$
* $C_2 = (U - V) / 2$

### Complexity Analysis
Because we have reduced the problem to strictly 2 recursive sub-problems of size $n/2$, and matrix addition/subtraction takes $O(n^2)$ time:
* **Recurrence Relation:** $T(n) = 2T(n/2) + O(n^2)$
* **Master Theorem:** According to Master Theorem Case 3, this evaluates to strictly **$O(n^2)$**.
